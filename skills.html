Skills (interactive): what the harness can do today

The chain. S2, a looking agent that sees only the robot's head camera, writes a plan: one or more calls, each a few keypoints of a thing. One skill program (version 3) turns every call into one reference that the frozen tracking policy S0 follows: the arm follows the wrist rows, S0 moves the fingers on every row. Before anything moves the program checks the reference for free: the arm planner, then S0 itself in a test bench. Grasping is always S0's own; no finger command is scripted.

Input of every skill. (1) S2's plan: one JSON file with one or more calls; the fields each skill reads are listed on its card. (2) The skill's own head-camera view at the start of the session, colour and depth: it locates the things itself, takes the thing nearest S2's first keypoint, reads its size and shape from its depth points and the support under the last keypoint, and plans the arm against that view. (3) The grasps the harness's proposer offers for that thing (recorded human clips). (4) Measured tables: the capability store (speeds, the release recipe, the wrist-turn rule), the clip library, the slide's speed table, the rehearsal file of the tight route. The command line holds the task folder, the plan file, a run name and the episode number: no flag per task. Output: for each call one reference for S0: the thing's pose, the wrist's pose and a frame of the recorded grasp for every row, 50 rows a second.

Transport: grasp, carry, set down

The first and main skill. S2 gives where the thing's middle should be along its way; the skill does everything else.

Input: what the skill reads from S2's plan

  • keypoints: 5 or 6 points (x, y, z in metres) of the thing's MIDDLE. The first is where it is now, and that is also how the skill knows WHICH thing: it takes the thing it sees itself nearest that point. The last is the middle at rest on the place, or at the moment of letting go into an opening
  • on a keypoint, only where it matters: axis (which end must be up) and heading_deg (a turn about the vertical)
  • shape: one word. The program acts on ball, block, tri, bar_lying, bar_upright; for any other word it uses its own reading of the thing's depth points. For a long lying thing also its outline
  • place: support_z (the support's height as S2 measured it), through and opening when the thing is let go into an opening, near (what stands beside)
  • NOT read by the program, though S2 writes them: the thing's number, size and what it stands on, how sure each number is, why, what was measured and assumed. They are S2's working and stay with the run

What the skill does

  • takes a grasp the harness's proposer offers (a recorded human clip), bound unchanged; the wrist is on the clip's own frames at the grasp
  • writes ONE reference for S0: the thing's pose and a clip frame for every row, one curve, one time law; the arm follows the wrist rows; S0 moves the fingers on every row
  • checks it for free before anything moves: the arm planner, then S0 itself in a test bench with training's randomisation (held to the release in 7 of 8, set down, let go at rest)
  • lets go by a measured form: the wrist still while the thing's reference sinks, or the grasp played backwards
  • if the plan as written is refused: tries the nearest feasible plan (let-go raised, landing short or sideways) and says what it changed

Measured

  • speed is not the limit: a carried cube and rod held 160 of 160 up to 0.8 m/s and 4 m/s2 (bench)
  • the grasp tolerates the palm less than 5 mm above the clip's height (45 of 45); 20 mm and more: 2 of 25
  • S0 never lets go if the reference just freezes (0 of 32); the grasp played backwards: placed 184 of 184 (cube)
  • the wrist turns a held thing at up to about 60 degrees a second; more than 60 degrees of a turn is done in the hand (the owner's rule)
  • where the thing ended in verified runs: the block 0.6 to 1.3 mm from S2's keypoint, the orange 19.6 mm
Where it stands. Verified passes: block tower (clean), candle, orange, sink plug (solid table only). Used as the first call of the drawer and triangle tasks.

t16, judged PASS: the 42 mm block taken from above and set on the tower. Measured from the record: the palm stayed within 1.1 mm of its reference; the thing was 2.7 mm from its reference at the release row. Run t16_block_tower_f14, 520 rows.

Push: move a thing or a front face along a line, without grasping

The second skill, added when transport alone could not finish a task (the owner: "if the only transportation is not enough ... introduce more skills").

Input: what the skill reads from S2's plan

  • keypoints: the first is the point of the pushed face the hand meets NOW (x, y, z); the last is the same point when the push is done. Their distance along the line is the travel (a middle point is written and not read)
  • direction: a unit vector; its horizontal part is the line (when missing: from the first point to the last)
  • place.sure_mm.travel: how sure S2 is of the travel. The skill takes the end for a stop: the wrist goes on past it by this much plus 11.5 mm (the fingertip beyond its joint)
  • NOT read yet, though S2 writes them: whether the end is a stop or movable, what stands beside and beyond it

What the skill does

  • runs after a transport in the same session with the clip that transport bound, or alone (then it binds a grasp the proposer offers for the pushed thing and never executes it): the hand keeps that clip's open posture, S0's fingers on every row
  • comes down behind the point and moves along the line; one reference, one plan with the pushed thing as the declared contact; then looks again

Measured

  • a drawer on rails: 135 mm at 0.04 m/s, 13.6 N while moving, 19 N at the stop (pushed by the little finger's first link, not by the fingertips)
  • a loose bar pushed up a slope moved 46 to 55 mm a call at best, and only at 0.01 m/s; such a call costs 720 to 930 of a session's 1500 rows
  • it cannot stop on first contact: it runs its line to the end
Where it stands. Verified pass: the jar into the drawer (transport), then the drawer pushed shut (3 of 3 runs). Not solved with it: the chock pushed against the drum.

t19, call 2 (push), judged PASS: the open hand comes behind the drawer front and pushes it 135 mm shut. The drawer itself is not drawn; the numbers mark the knob's tip, as S2 gave it. Measured from the record: the palm stayed within 1.5 mm of its reference; the thing was 1.8 mm from its reference at the release row. Run t19_jar_drawer_f14, 323 rows.

Press: a push whose line goes down

One fingertip comes over a point, goes down by the travel and comes back up. For a switch or a button.

Input: what the skill reads from S2's plan

  • a push call whose direction points down (its downward part over 0.26; [0, 0, -1] is straight down): that is what makes it a press
  • keypoints: the first is the point to press, as it is now (x, y, z)
  • travel_m: how far the point goes in (when missing: from the first point to the last along the line)
  • place.sure_mm.travel: the fingertip goes that much further than the travel

What the skill does

  • rehearses the fingertip's aim in the bench until S0's fingertip reads within 3 mm of the point, then presses once and looks again
  • can be the only call of a plan

Measured

  • it meets the thing about 10 mm from the point asked and may slide about 14 mm on a smooth face
  • a rocker switch latched at 4 N; the press gives at most about 11 to 15 N: it did not move a tilted paint-tin lid at all
  • a target smaller than the fingertip (an 8 mm key) is a guess
Where it stands. Verified pass: the power strip's switch (marginal: it latched 0.8 mm past the switch's painted face). Failed: the paint lid (needs a blow), the TV remote's 8 mm key.

t41, judged PASS: one fingertip comes over the switch, goes down by the travel and comes back up; the thing's reference does not move. The thing drawn is the power strip. Measured from the record: the palm stayed within 0.9 mm of its reference; the thing was 2.7 mm from its reference at the release row. Run t41_power_strip_f14, 150 rows.

A held tool that works: the thing's attitude follows every keypoint (new 2026-11-28, not passing yet)

For levering, tipping over a place and standing back, a turn made late on the way. Before, the program read only the first and the last keypoint's axis; now the thing is carried through every attitude S2 states, in order.

Input: what the skill reads from S2's plan

  • a transport call in which keypoints BETWEEN the first and the last state an axis other than the end's (where the thing's own direction must point there)
  • for a tool: the last 3 or 4 keypoints are the work itself (the working end brought to the spot, then rocked or moved), within about 8 cm of the last keypoint, and place.through true: the tool may touch things there and is let go after

What the skill does

  • carries the thing through the stated attitudes, a smooth step between two of them along the curve; the wrist carries all of the turning, the palm stays rigid on the thing
  • slows the carry until every piece's turning is inside the measured mean turn rate
  • everything else as a transport: one reference, the arm planner, the free check, the measured let-go

Measured

  • a held 50 x 70 mm cylinder tipped and brought back (bench, 8 copies, no randomisation): 60 degrees kept 8 of 8, 90 degrees 7 to 8, 120 degrees 0 to 4, 150 and 180 degrees 0. Under training's randomisation: 60 degrees 4 to 6 of 8, 90 degrees 3 to 4
  • a lever with a screwdriver (the paint lid): the tool stayed in S0's fingers through the stroke, its tip in the lid's notch with 23 N on the rim; the lid rose, but not far enough by the stroke alone
  • the fingers give a held tool little force: no finger link over 6.5 N in that stroke
Where it stands. No counted pass. The paint-lid lever was judged PASS three times and REFUTED by the verifier: a 38 mm step in the program's own wrist motion at the end of the stroke knocked the lid open. The step is removed (freeze f20); the same plan is then judged FAIL, and so are the nail pulled with the claw and the rolling pin pressed over the crackers.

A turn in the hand: the wrist still, the thing's reference turned (new 2026-11-28, built, nothing run yet)

For screwing, unscrewing and knobs. Learned from the recorded clips: 43 stretches of in-hand turning in 28 clips of 21 object classes.

Input: what the skill reads from S2's plan

  • in the transport call: "turn": {"turns": n, "about": [x, y, z], "where": "at_the_place" or "before_lifting", "advance_mm": the thread's way}; turns is signed (positive = anticlockwise seen from the tip of about)

What the skill does

  • takes as its grasp a recorded twirl's own clip (one that begins on the table, whose thing stands as this one stands and is twirled about the asked axis), laid on the thing; plays it from its grasp to the frame the person begins to twirl
  • carries the thing to the place (or stays, for a turn before lifting), then adds rows in which the wrist's reference is still and the thing's reference turns about the axis through its middle at 60 degrees a second; then lets go as a transport does
  • free check: the landing's own criterion AND the thing turned by 75 percent of the asked angle in as many copies

Measured

  • from a recorded twirl's entry grasp a generated turn of 2 turns at 60 degrees a second is followed whole, both ways: light bulb 8 of 8 (two clips), 60 mm cylinder 8 of 8 (bench, no randomisation)
  • from the PICK grasp of the same clips it is not: bulb 0 of 8 in five of six jobs. The grasp decides
  • a screwdriver: 2 turns at 60 degrees a second 14 of 16 from the twirl grasp; at 90 degrees a second over 4 turns most copies stall near 2 turns
  • the recorded twirls do not start where the tasks' things stand: the bulbs' begin with the bulb standing on its glass, the thin rods' with the hand under the thing (recorded on a stand)
Where it stands. No run yet: every plan with a turn was refused before anything moved. Hob knob: the recorded grasp's fingertips come 0.3 to 4.6 mm from the rim of the knob's seat (the planner asks 5 mm). Tube bulb in the lamp and table leg: the only twirls of an upright rod have the hand under it.

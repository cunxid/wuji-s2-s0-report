Transport: grasp, carry, set down

The first and main skill. S2 gives where the thing's middle should be along its way; the skill does everything else.

What S2 gives

  • 5 or 6 keypoints of the thing's MIDDLE from where it is to where it must rest, each anchored to something measured in the head view
  • the thing's shape word, which end is up (an axis) and a heading where they matter
  • the place: the support's height, how sure, what stands near, and whether the thing is set down or let go into an opening

What the skill does

  • takes a grasp the harness's proposer offers (a recorded human clip), bound unchanged; the wrist is on the clip's own frames at the grasp
  • writes ONE reference for S0: the thing's pose and a clip frame for every row, one curve, one time law; the arm follows the wrist rows; S0 moves the fingers on every row
  • checks it for free before anything moves: the arm planner, then S0 itself in a test bench with training's randomisation (held to the release in 7 of 8, set down, let go at rest)
  • lets go by a measured form: the wrist still while the thing's reference sinks, or the grasp played backwards
  • if the plan as written is refused: tries the nearest feasible plan (let-go raised, landing short or sideways) and says what it changed

Measured

  • speed is not the limit: a carried cube and rod held 160 of 160 up to 0.8 m/s and 4 m/s2 (bench)
  • the grasp tolerates the palm less than 5 mm above the clip's height (45 of 45); 20 mm and more: 2 of 25
  • S0 never lets go if the reference just freezes (0 of 32); the grasp played backwards: placed 184 of 184 (cube)
  • the wrist turns a held thing at up to about 60 degrees a second; more than 60 degrees of a turn is done in the hand (the owner's rule)
  • set-down accuracy in verified runs: block 0.6 to 1.3 mm from the keypoint, candle 0.1 mm, orange 19.6 mm
Where it stands. Verified passes: block tower (clean), candle, orange, sink plug (solid table only). Used as the first call of the drawer and triangle tasks.

t16, judged PASS: the 42 mm block taken from above and set on the tower. Measured from the record: the palm stayed within 1.1 mm of its reference; the thing was 2.7 mm from its reference at the release row. Run t16_block_tower_f14, 520 rows.

Transport of a thing that rolls (a route inside transport)

Chosen by rule when S2's shape word is 'ball'. A ball that is let go while it still moves rolls away, so the release is different.

What S2 gives

  • the same as a transport; the shape word 'ball' selects the route

What the skill does

  • starts the session in the command mode in which S0 is SHOWN the reference fingers (mode full)
  • releases with the wrist still while the reference fingers open; its bench check has a mechanism term: the last finger is off BEFORE the wrist rises

Measured

  • in the other command mode S0 never opens on a ball while the wrist stands (bench, 0 of 8); in mode full with the fingers shown opening it does (mechanism term 8 of 8 in the run's own check)
  • the orange ends on the pan 19.6 mm from the keypoint, let go at 0.03 m/s: real, not clean
Where it stands. Verified pass: the orange on the scale (3 of 3 runs).

t49, judged PASS: the orange carried to the scale's pan and let go with the wrist still. Measured from the record: the palm stayed within 0.6 mm of its reference; the thing was 14.2 mm from its reference at the release row. Run t49_weigh_orange_f8, 676 rows.

Transport into a tight opening, after a rehearsal on a twin scene (a route inside transport)

Chosen by rule when S2's place is an opening the thing goes through with less than 5 mm of room a side. Where a thing sits in the fingers is off by centimetres and cannot be predicted from another scene, so the skill measures it first.

What S2 gives

  • the same as a transport, with the place marked 'through' and the opening's room a side as a number

What the skill does

  • builds a twin scene from its own head view (a bare table, a library block at the seen pose, S2's opening as a lid)
  • rehearses the same grasp and carry on the twin, up to four times; a script writes the measured offset of the thing in the hand, a release correction and S0's own finger posture into a file
  • when a rehearsal goes through the twin's opening, runs the real task once with that file

Measured

  • the twin's block is 0.5 mm from the true pose; the offset measured on the twin and in the task differ by 1.9 mm and 1.0 degree
  • the block was 0.3 to 0.5 mm from its aim before the release in the two verified runs
  • thin: rehearsals went through 1 of 5 on the solid table and 1 of 3 in the new environment before the task run
Where it stands. Verified pass: the triangle block through the shape sorter (2 mm of room a side), on the solid table and in the new environment (3 of 3 runs there).

First rehearsal on the twin scene, with the bench's guess of where the block sits in the hand: it ends wedged in the opening at about 33 degrees. Measured from the record: the palm stayed within 0.9 mm of its reference; the thing was 29.1 mm from its reference at the release row. Run t17_shape_sorter_f6_r1, 689 rows.

Push: move a thing or a front face along a line, without grasping

The second skill, added when transport alone could not finish a task (the owner: "if the only transportation is not enough ... introduce more skills").

What S2 gives

  • the point the hand is to meet (a drawer front, a knob), where that point must end, the line, the travel and how sure it is
  • whether the end is a stop; what stands beside and beyond it

What the skill does

  • runs after a transport in the same session with the clip that transport bound, or alone (then it binds a grasp the proposer offers for the pushed thing and never executes it): the hand keeps that clip's open posture, S0's fingers on every row
  • comes down behind the point and moves along the line; one reference, one plan with the pushed thing as the declared contact; then looks again

Measured

  • a drawer on rails: 135 mm at 0.04 m/s, 13.6 N while moving, 19 N at the stop (pushed by the base link of the little finger, not the fingertips)
  • a loose bar on a slope follows only at 0.01 m/s and about half the commanded way: too slow for one session's tick budget
  • it cannot stop on first contact: it runs its line to the end
Where it stands. Verified pass: the jar into the drawer (transport), then the drawer pushed shut (3 of 3 runs). Not solved with it: the chock pushed against the drum.

t19, call 1 (transport): the jar carried and let go into the open drawer. Measured from the record: the palm stayed within 1.0 mm of its reference; the thing was 11.6 mm from its reference at the release row. Run t19_jar_drawer_f14, 726 rows.

Press: a push whose line goes down

One fingertip comes over a point, goes down by the travel and comes back up. For a switch or a button.

What S2 gives

  • the point to press, the direction (down), the travel in mm and how sure, what is within 15 mm, what kind of end it is (a click, a stop)

What the skill does

  • rehearses the fingertip's aim in the bench until S0's fingertip reads within 3 mm of the point, then presses once and looks again
  • can be the only call of a plan

Measured

  • it meets the thing about 10 mm from the point asked and may slide about 14 mm on a smooth face
  • a rocker switch latched at 4 N; the press gives at most about 11 to 15 N: it did not move a tilted paint-tin lid at all
  • a target smaller than the fingertip (an 8 mm key) is a guess
Where it stands. Verified pass: the power strip's switch (marginal: it latched 0.8 mm past the switch's painted face). Failed: the paint lid (needs a blow), the TV remote's 8 mm key.

t41, judged PASS: one fingertip comes over the switch, goes down by the travel and comes back up; the thing's reference does not move. The thing drawn is the power strip. Measured from the record: the palm stayed within 0.9 mm of its reference; the thing was 2.7 mm from its reference at the release row. Run t41_power_strip_f14, 150 rows.

Slide: a transport whose thing stays on a surface (built, not passing yet)

For wiping or scraping. Chosen by rule when S2's plan says the thing keeps touching a named surface.

What S2 gives

  • a transport whose keypoints all lie on the surface, with 'keep touching', the surface, and how high the thing's middle rides over it

What the skill does

  • grasps as usual, then moves the thing along the surface with its underside flat on the plane the skill fits to what it sees; the stroke's speed comes from a bench table

Measured

  • on a board propped at 12 degrees the eraser lay flat on all 163 ticks of the stroke and followed 169.5 of 168.7 mm at 0.10 m/s
  • S0 kept a dragged bar to the release in 13, 15, 15, 13, 13 of 16 at 0.03, 0.06, 0.10, 0.15, 0.20 m/s
  • NOT SOLVED: the let-go. With the wrist still S0 takes its last finger off only after the wrist begins to rise (0 of 8); with the grasp played backwards the hand takes the thing back (placed 0 of 8)
Where it stands. No pass. The whiteboard run below was judged FAIL: the stroke is right, then the leaving hand lifted the eraser 66 mm. The skill now refuses such a run before anything moves.

t39, judged FAIL: watch the right view at the end: the stroke is on the board, then the eraser is lifted with the leaving hand and falls back. What no skill does yet

  • Let a slid thing go where it lies (the whiteboard).
  • Lay a low flat thing down within 10 mm (the TV remote: held in about 70 percent of bench tries, slips about 25 mm in the palm).
  • Pick a low thing with sloped sides from a SOLID table (the wheel chock: 0 of 2128 in a bench with a solid ground; possible only because the test environment lets the hand pass through the table, as training did).
  • Reach into space the head camera cannot see: the arm planner treats it as occupied (the microwave).
  • A blow, a screw, a squeeze, a turn of a held thing of 90 degrees or more.

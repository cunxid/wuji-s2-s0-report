S2 proposes its own goals and decomposes them on the measured boundary

The claim, made testable: given only a scene (MuJoCo model structure — bodies, joints, geoms, sizes; object names as a perception proxy; never the task name or success function), S2 (1) generates candidate goals from an affordance grammar, and (2) decomposes each into registered skill chains, gating every step on the measured capability atlas and kinematic reach — emitting a feasibility prediction with the limiting reason (s2/dexharness/propose.py). Ground truth (benchmark-intended goals + our measured campaign outcomes) lives only in the evaluator (eval_propose.py).

Error analysis (the honest part)

All 4 optimistic predictions share one blind spot: the predictor models grasp class + reach but not occlusion or articulation dynamics — glasses spring back, the microwave body occludes its own button, the ipad needs a cross-body or left-hand press. 2 pessimistic: opaque object identity (the watering can is link_2; vessel-shape perception would fix it) and the relocated tongs. 2 recovery misses: deformable cloth (outside the rigid-object grammar — flex bodies now detected but this scene's cloth is composite) and the far-spot base mouse. It also proposes goals never in any benchmark (press stop_button, spin/pour/handoff variants per object) — the generator is a superset of the benchmarks.

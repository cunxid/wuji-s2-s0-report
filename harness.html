Inside the harness

The test environment (the owner's order of 2026-11-26: the same as S0's training environment):

  • the hand's physics as in training: its contact friction (0.7), finger torque, joint ranges and masses;
  • the hand does not collide with the table or with anything fixed to it. In training the hand passes through the ground, and S0 learned to hook its fingertips 9 to 25 mm under a low thing. With a solid ground a lying triangular bar that S0 picks 16 of 16 times is picked 0 of 16 times;
  • the arm is as stiff at the palm as training's wrist (15 N/mm; it was 3 N/mm);
  • still not as in training: an arm in place of six wrist servos, a real scene around the thing, no randomisation, and S0 is shown the surface of the recorded grasp's object where training showed it the held thing's own (in the bench a can was kept 8 of 8 times when shown its own surface and 2 of 8 when shown the other; a switch for this is built and not tested).

What it is held to. S2 sees the head camera and the joint readings only, and gets the judged word only. No grasp is scripted: the fingers are S0's on every row. A reference is one piece (never grasp, lift and carry chained); one recorded grasp per session; a turn of more than 60 degrees is done in the hand. No number read off a task's own attempts is in the program, and a free check's criterion is fixed before its run. Ten of the fifty tasks are held out and were never played.

Where it stands (2026-11-27 20:38, nothing running). 16 of the 50 tasks were tried. 6 pass with the one program, each read by a refuting verifier: the block tower, the candle, the orange, the jar and drawer (a transport, then a push), the triangle through the shape sorter (twin rehearsal), the power strip (one press). The program's last freeze (the fourteenth, 18:22) passed the block tower, the drawer and the power strip again; the candle, the orange and the triangle last passed on the eighth freeze of the same day, and what changed since then is the slide. 10 tasks fail or are refused before anything moves, each with a measured reason on its page. In all 263 runs: 78 judged PASS, 50 judged FAIL, 135 refused by the program or not judged. These are development runs, not first attempts and not a counted series.

3 · Motion-planning gates around every skill call

Wiring status: checks (i) and (ii) run automatically inside the compile path; check (iii) is a standalone tool today — it was run after the fact on the traced references and used once as a forward planner (the lying-cup approach) — it does not yet block execution by default. The checks, in order: (i) the yaw-sweep kinematic IK search (endpoint feasibility over candidate spawn yaws, warm-started damped-least-squares on a scratch model); (ii) the depth corridor check (swept path vs measured height map); (iii) the IK path gate: full-path 50 Hz IK with joint-velocity/acceleration bounds, manipulability (singularity proximity) and joint-limit margins — references that would demand accel spikes or pass near singular configs are rejected with a typed reason or returned with a minimal retime factor.

Calibration: withdrawn. The gate was run on 23 composed references from the shipped rig: 13 whose episodes had been reported as passes (the "winners") and 10 reported as failures. Its thresholds were set to the envelope of the winners plus 15%, so that the gate would not reject them (the two mouse-press references were left out of that envelope and rejected; see below). The audit has since examined all 13 winner rows: 10 are FAIL, 3 are demos, none is a pass. The three demo rows are the bucket run, which is listed under two row names, and the cone turn. The thresholds therefore describe references that were run, not references that succeed. As recorded in s2/s2loop/ik_gate_study.json, they allow an IK residual of up to 235 mm (set by the hammer-strike reference) and a swept penetration of up to 32 mm (set by the peg reference, whose wrist flange passes 31 mm into the table). Both of those rows are audited FAIL. The earlier statements "13/13 winners pass" and "predicts 4 of 10 real failures before spending an episode" are withdrawn as measures of how well the gate separates success from failure: no reference the gate accepts belongs to a run that passed, so being accepted says nothing about whether the task succeeds. The gate has not been recalibrated.

What still stands are the gate's kinematic measurements of individual references, which do not depend on any score. Of the 10 references reported as failures it rejects four, and all four episodes were failures when run: water-plant's 78° sustained orientation unreachability, pinch-tongs 428 mm out of workspace (57% solver-recovery dwell), the microwave press (375 mm/178°), and t09's overshooting aim, whose wrist flange sweeps 73 mm into the table. The t09 aim that had been called the winner sweeps the same flange 31 mm into the table and is accepted; the audit found the arm 6–7 cm off its reference through that set-down. A per-family comparison: the allen-key spin reference carries a sustained IK residual of 21 mm/49°, against ≤0.1 mm for the three spin references that had been reported as passes (after the audit: the cone turn is a demo with medium confidence, the paintbrush flip a FAIL, and the jar-lid row a FAIL that re-ran the screwdriver roll).

The one defect the gate found, and what the audit added. The gate flagged the mouse-press reference. The press composer's frame anchor came from a sim-binding stub that free-fell through the floor during settle, so the authored march spanned metres of junk space, and the episode reached the button only via the arrival-gated replan. The composer now detects the degenerate frame and re-anchors to the hand's own pose. After the fix the same click episode presses the button further (15.4 mm of button travel, up from 13.0), its authored plan passes the gate with 0 out-of-workspace frames, and arenas with a live tracked object are bit-identical to full float precision. That click, made in a rebuilt scene, is one of the four behaviours that hold up as a demo; it is the click only, not the move-and-click task. This page described the fix as "at root cause"; that is withdrawn. The fix changed the press composer only. The audit found the same defect in the push composer, which takes the table height from the stub's settled position: the t14 push reference sat about 28 m below the table and the arm was in IK recovery for the whole push. That reference was not among the 23 the gate was run on. Fixed in the code on 2026-11-23, after the audit: when the tracked placeholder object has fallen out of the scene, the co-simulation now anchors its frame on the hand instead, and the push takes the table height from the body it pushes. Before and after on the audited push in the same scene: table height read as −27.70 m, now 0.890 m; arm error at the start 28 585 mm, now 24 mm; the arm is no longer dragged over the mouse before the stroke. Two ordinary episodes (a place and a push of the tracked object) give identical results before and after the change. This is a repair of the reference only, not a pass: the re-run push still ends 606 mm from its target, because the target it was given lies outside the arm's reach (it is not the mouse pad), and the arm error grows to 200–400 mm once the stroke leaves the workspace.

Blind spots. Pure grasp slips and semantic failures are kinematically clean, so the gate cannot see them. The earlier statement that the atlas can see them is withdrawn for semantic failures: the atlas records what the scores report, and the audit found those scores passing runs that did not do the task. The audited runs show a third blind spot: the arm can sit far from a reference that a kinematic solve calls reachable. In the hammer face-to-claw run the wrist stayed about 10 cm off its commanded pose although a kinematics-only solve of the same reference raises no flag, and in the paintbrush run the palm drifted up to 51 mm off a reference the gate scores at 0.02 mm residual.

Retime and forward use. Retime example under a tight accel budget: the t25 and t09 references would be slowed by ×1.68 and ×1.07. These are computed factors; no episode was run with them, and both rows are audited FAIL. The gate has also been used forward, as a planner: its swept-clearance table (4 candidate approaches, zero episodes) was used to choose the t19 lying-cup approach — stock approach −12.9 mm penetration, gate-selected offset +29.6 mm clearance — after which the cup was lifted in 5 of 5 acquires. That result was not audited: "lifted" means the object rose more than 5 cm, and every one of those carries then dropped the cup (section 5). The same study measured that spawn-yaw CANNOT change the hand-vs-object approach (the clip anchors rigidly to the object) — the measured-offset CORRECT is the approach lever. API: ik_gate.check_reference(arena, poses_50hz, arm).

4 · The agent between skills — measured closed loop

After every verb segment the pipeline re-observes with the real perception stack (median 1.8 s per boundary), judges, and routes: continue · one measured-correction retry in the validated 12–80 mm band · typed abort. The judge decides from perception wherever the effect shows in the depth image (is the object held, where is it, is the bowl occupied); ground truth is recorded alongside and not used for those decisions. Nail travel, button travel and the pour tilt angle are not resolved by perception, so for those the loop defers to the episode's own score.

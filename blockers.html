The 40 tasks tried: where each one stops

What this page is. Every playable in-house task (40; ten more are held out and never played) has now been tried with the one skill program: S2 looks through the head camera and writes keypoints, the program makes one reference, checks it for free (the arm planner, then S0 itself in a test bench with training's randomisation) and runs it; every judged PASS is read by a refuting verifier before it counts. 7 pass. For the other 33 the table says how far the program got and what stops it, in the coordinator's words; the runs behind each line are on the task's own page. Development runs, not first attempts and not a counted series.

What would move the count, and whose call it is

  • The free check's bar (the owner's): it asks 7 of 8 or 14 of 16 copies under training's randomisation. Several references that fail it hold 8 of 8 without randomisation (a 90 degree tip, a generated pick), and a session has no randomisation. Running such plans as labelled trials needs the owner's word; a copy of the program that ignores the check was refused by the permission system and was not worked around.
  • Clearances missed by under a millimetre (the owner's): the planner asks 5 mm of the arm and the wrist against everything seen, the table included; t24, t25 and t21 stop 0.1 to 0.5 mm inside it.
  • Set down, then push into place (new 2026-11-28): where the place has no room for the hand or is a loose thing, S2 now sets the thing down beside it and pushes it in. It gave the paperweight its pass; whether a thing pushed onto its place counts where the task says 'put' is the owner's to rule.
  • Force and speed: eight tasks need a blow, a hard press or a squeeze. The policy's fingers give about 6 N each on a held tool. This is S0's, not the harness's.
  • Turns in the hand: the skill is built and bench-proven from the recorded twirl grasps; it needs recorded twirls that start as the tasks' things stand (a bulb lying, a rod standing on a table with the hand above it), or a pick generated into the twirl pose that survives randomisation.
  • Aim of a tool's working end: the rehearsal on a twin scene that solved the 2 mm opening could be built for tools.
